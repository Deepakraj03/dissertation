\chapter{Future Plan}
\label{ch:futureplan}

This chapter sets out the work remaining to complete the dissertation, in priority order, and how each item connects back to the objectives in Section~1.2 and the gaps Chapter~5 (Section~\ref{sec:synthesis}) left open.

\section{Reconcile the Proposed Architecture Against What Was Actually Built}
\label{sec:reconcile}

Chapter~3 specifies a physics-constrained Neural Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge (UNSB) with four physics-informed loss terms as Stage~2. What Chapter~5 reports instead is a loss-tuned CycleGAN for restyling (Track~1), a deterministic geometric renderer with no learned component, a single-image DTM estimator, and a separately-trained ControlNet for texture synthesis (Track~3) --- a different architecture family, arrived at because the corpus-diversity diagnosis in Chapter~5 (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}) pointed at an explicit-geometry redesign, not because UNSB itself was found lacking. This divergence has not yet been reconciled against the approved methodology, and doing so is now higher-leverage than any further experiment: it is a two-way fork, not a single task.

\textbf{Option A --- implement UNSB as specified.} Build the entropy-regularised optimal-transport bridge and the four physics losses (Chapter~3, Equations~\ref{eq:sb_ot}--\ref{eq:l_total}) on top of the now-validated geometry-mediated corpus, treating Tracks~1--2 as the empirical baseline UNSB is meant to improve on, exactly as Chapter~3 (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}) originally intended. This keeps Methodology unchanged but is the larger remaining engineering task by a wide margin, and the three auxiliary loss networks (shadow-direction, Hapke inversion, geological classifier) would each need training data and evaluation of their own before the main bridge could be assessed.

\textbf{Option B --- formally adopt the geometry-mediated pipeline as a documented deviation.} Rewrite Chapter~3's Stage~2 architecture section to describe the renderer + DTM estimator + ControlNet pipeline actually built, with an explicit rationale section explaining why: the corpus-diversity finding made an explicit geometric intermediate necessary regardless of which learned architecture sits either side of it, and Track~3's paired-supervision texture synthesis is arguably a more direct fit to a Mars corpus this small than an unpaired bridge objective would be. This is less additional engineering, but requires being explicit in the dissertation's own words that the design changed and why, rather than the two chapters silently describing different systems.

Neither option should be assumed by default; which is chosen determines how much of the remaining timeline (Section~\ref{sec:timeline}) is architecture implementation versus consolidation and evaluation of what already exists.

\section{Decide Track 2's Corpus-Diversity Limitation}
\label{sec:track2-decision}

Track~2 (Chapter~5, Section~\ref{sec:track2}) validated that geometry-mediation breaks the near-identity shortcut, but its output diversity is compressed by an 8-terrain source corpus. Three defensible paths follow, and any of the three is a reasonable dissertation position provided it is stated and justified rather than left implicit:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accept it as a documented limitation.} Report the compression honestly against the corpus's real constraint (only 8 stereo DTMs exist for the landing region at all) and argue the shortcut-breaking result stands on its own.
  \item \textbf{Scale the corpus.} Widen the DTM search beyond the strict landing-region bounding box (accepting some geographic content dilution, the same trade-off Chapter~4, Section~\ref{sec:renderer-impl} made in the other direction) or increase per-DTM crop density beyond the current 200-per-product cap.
  \item \textbf{Change the Stage~2 restyling architecture.} A diffusion-based or transformer-based generator conditioned more directly on the render than CycleGAN's cycle-consistency objective allows might resist the shared-texture-template failure mode more effectively; this option is more entangled with Section~\ref{sec:reconcile}'s fork than the other two.
\end{itemize}

\section{Close or Formally Cut Stage 1 (DiffusionSR)}
\label{sec:diffusionsr-decision}

The CTX/HiRISE co-registration pipeline for Stage~1 (nadir super-resolution) has not been touched since the very first day of the project: a five-image smoke test succeeded, and it has not progressed since, while the geometry-mediated Stage~1.5 work (Chapter~4, Sections~\ref{sec:dtmestimator-impl} onward) proceeded on the assumption that HiRISE coverage is already available at the primary test sites, which Chapter~3 (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}) notes makes Stage~1 optional there. Two honest paths: run the full co-registration batch and fold Stage~1 into the final system for sites without native HiRISE coverage, or formally scope it out of the submitted dissertation with a stated reason (limited remaining time, and Stage~1.5/2/3 being the parts Objective~O2 actually depends on). A month-old, silently unstarted thread is a worse look in the final document than either choice made explicitly.

\section{Extend the Evaluation Framework Beyond FID and RMSE}
\label{sec:eval-extend}

Chapter~3's evaluation framework (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) specifies six measurements; to date only FID (Chapter~5, Tables~\ref{tab:fid-results}, \ref{tab:track1-fid}) and the DTM estimator's RMSE (Section~\ref{sec:dtm-results}, not itself part of the original framework) have been computed even once. Shadow-angle error is the cheapest of the four untouched metrics to add next: HiRISE products already carry solar azimuth/elevation metadata, and the render pipeline (Chapter~4, Section~\ref{sec:renderer-impl}) already produces a geometrically exact synthetic scene to compare a generated image's apparent shadow direction against. LPIPS is a near-free addition alongside FID once a structural reference exists (the geometry corpus's own renders can serve as that reference for Track~2). Hapke BRDF residual and hallucination rate both require the auxiliary networks referenced in Section~\ref{sec:reconcile}'s Option~A, and are realistically gated on that decision.

\section{Scope Stage 3 (Gaussian Splatting) Explicitly}
\label{sec:gs-decision}

No code exists yet for the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering stage Chapter~3 (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}) specifies as Stage~3, and Objective~O3's $\geq$72\,fps VR target has not been assessed at all. Given the remaining timeline, this should be scoped in or out of the final submission explicitly now, since the decision shapes how much of Stage~2's output needs to be multi-view-consistent (several perspective renders of the same scene from nearby poses) versus evaluatable as single images, which is all Chapter~5 has produced so far.

\section{Indicative Timeline}
\label{sec:timeline}

Reconciling the architecture (Section~\ref{sec:reconcile}) and bringing this document current are the immediate priorities, since every other item's scope depends on which of Options~A/B is chosen. Deciding Track~2's corpus fate and DiffusionSR's disposition follow directly and are comparatively cheap to resolve. Extending the evaluation framework should begin with shadow-angle error alongside whichever Stage~2 work Section~\ref{sec:reconcile} leads to, rather than waiting for it to finish. Gaussian Splatting, if scoped in, is the largest remaining net-new engineering task and would follow once Stage~2 output is stable; if scoped out, time is reserved instead for a full Results/Evaluation pass against the extended metric set and for the Conclusions chapter, neither of which this draft yet includes.
