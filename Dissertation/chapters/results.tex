\chapter{CycleGAN Baseline: Training, Evaluation and Diagnosis}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports the results of training the CycleGAN baseline described in Chapter~4 on the corrected, full-resolution HiRISE corpus, evaluates it quantitatively and visually, and presents a specific, evidence-based diagnosis of why it currently falls well short of the target FID --- rather than a general appeal to GAN training instability.

\section{Training Run}

A 50-epoch run (batch size 4, learning rate $2{\times}10^{-4}$ constant for the first 25 epochs then linearly decayed to zero, $\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}}=10$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{idt}}=5$) was carried out on the A4000, taking approximately 37 minutes per epoch and $\sim$31 hours in total. Figure~\ref{fig:losscurves} shows the full loss history. The generator and cycle-consistency losses converge smoothly throughout --- on their own, they would read as a healthy training run. The discriminator losses tell a different story: $D_H$ (the discriminator judging HiRISE realism) settles into a stable equilibrium around 0.03 from epoch~10 onward, while $D_R$ (the discriminator judging rover-image realism) starts \emph{higher} than $D_H$ at epoch~1 (0.162 vs.\ 0.095), crosses below it by epoch~3, and then decays smoothly and monotonically for the rest of training, reaching $3.99{\times}10^{-6}$ by epoch~50 (Table~\ref{tab:disc-timeline}). This divergence is visible well before the learning-rate decay begins at epoch~26, so it is not an artefact of the training schedule.

\section{Visual Inspection of Sample Translations}
\label{sec:visual}

Sample translation grids saved during training (each showing real HiRISE~$\to$~translated-to-rover~$\to$~reconstructed-HiRISE on top, and real rover~$\to$~translated-to-HiRISE~$\to$~reconstructed-rover below) confirm the same pattern at every epoch inspected, from epoch~1 through the final epoch~50 checkpoint (Figures~\ref{fig:sample001}--\ref{fig:sample050}). The HiRISE-to-rover translation stays a grainy, high-frequency echo of the input with no rover-ground structure; the rover-to-HiRISE translation stays a washed-out, low-contrast ghost of the input rather than adopting HiRISE's characteristic appearance. Both reconstructions (cycle-consistency columns) match their originals closely at every epoch --- but, as Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis} argues, that is a symptom of the underlying problem rather than evidence against it.

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

The evidence in Sections~4.1--4.3 converges on a specific, mechanistic explanation rather than a generic ``GANs are unstable'' account.

$D_R$ judges whether a given image is a genuine rover photograph; it is the discriminator that tells $G_{H2R}$ (the HiRISE-to-rover generator) whether its output is convincing. The corrected HiRISE corpus (Chapter~4) is genuinely grainy --- visible sensor speckle from real 16-bit radiometric data, stretched to 8-bit --- while real rover NAVCAM photographs are comparatively smooth, well-exposed camera images. That is a low-level \emph{noise-statistics} gap layered on top of the intended \emph{style} gap the network is meant to learn to bridge, and it is a far easier signal for a convolutional discriminator to key on than genuine cross-domain semantics (crater texture versus rock/dust texture): $D_R$ can reject $G_{H2R}$'s output purely because it still carries HiRISE-style grain, without ever needing to learn what a real rover photograph actually looks like. Once $D_R$ locks onto that shortcut --- by epoch~3, per Table~\ref{tab:disc-timeline} --- it wins for the rest of training essentially for free, so $G_{H2R}$ receives a real but semantically uninformative gradient, consistent with its output staying a grainy echo of the input at every epoch inspected (Section~\ref{sec:visual}). The reverse direction, $G_{R2H}$ turning a smooth rover image grainy, is the easier direction for a generator to fake at least superficially, which is consistent with $D_H$ remaining in a healthier, contested equilibrium throughout.

This account also explains why correcting the corpus resolution made FID worse rather than better (Table~\ref{tab:fid-results}): the earlier, browse-resolution corpus was JPEG-compressed and resampled, and therefore smoother and closer to rover-image smoothness, so it did not hand $D_R$ this shortcut as blatantly. Fixing the data-quality bug was still the correct thing to do --- training and evaluating against genuinely wrong-resolution imagery would have made every subsequent result meaningless --- but it surfaced a training-dynamics problem that the smoother, wrong corpus had been partially masking.

\section{Candidate Fixes}

Three candidate fixes follow directly from the diagnosis, in rough order of expected leverage versus implementation effort:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Close the noise-statistics gap directly}, either by applying mild denoising to real HiRISE patches or by adding matched synthetic sensor grain to the rover domain, so that $D_R$ can no longer separate real from generated rover images on texture statistics alone and is forced to engage with genuine content.
  \item \textbf{Weaken $D_R$ specifically} relative to the generators --- a lower learning rate or a reduced update frequency for $D_R$ alone --- which treats the symptom directly and is the cheapest to try.
  \item \textbf{Reduce $\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ below 10}, so that cycle-consistency stops rewarding a near-identity mapping and $G_{H2R}$ is less anchored to reproducing input structure.
\end{enumerate}

Resolving this diagnosis is the immediate next priority, ahead of building the physics-constrained UNSB architecture Chapter~3 specifies, since a well-understood, correctly-balanced CycleGAN baseline is a more useful point of comparison for that later work than one left in its current, diagnosed-but-unresolved state.
